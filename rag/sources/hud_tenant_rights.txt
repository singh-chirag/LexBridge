U.S. DEPARTMENT OF HOUSING AND URBAN DEVELOPMENT - TENANT RIGHTS

BASIC TENANT PROTECTIONS

HABITABILITY
Landlords must provide and maintain housing that is:
- Structurally sound and weatherproof
- Equipped with working heat, water, and electricity
- Free from health and safety hazards
- Compliant with local housing codes

If repairs are needed, tenants should:
1. Notify landlord in writing
2. Allow reasonable time for repairs
3. Check local laws about "repair and deduct" or rent withholding

EVICTION PROTECTIONS
Landlords generally cannot evict tenants without:
- Proper written notice (typically 3-30 days depending on reason)
- A valid legal reason (non-payment, lease violation, etc.)
- A court order (self-help evictions are illegal)

Tenants facing eviction have the right to:
- Receive proper notice as required by state law
- Appear in court and present a defense
- Request a continuance to find legal help

SECURITY DEPOSITS
Most states require landlords to:
- Limit deposit amounts (often 1-2 months' rent)
- Return deposits within 14-60 days after move-out
- Provide itemized deductions for any withheld amounts
- Keep deposits in separate, interest-bearing accounts

FAIR HOUSING
The Fair Housing Act prohibits discrimination based on:
- Race, color, national origin
- Religion, sex, familial status
- Disability

If you believe you've been discriminated against:
- File a complaint with HUD: hud.gov/complaints
- Contact a fair housing organization in your area
